About me

How I got into finance

I'm from the Coral Gables and Coconut Grove area of Miami. Most of my childhood was spent on a baseball field or on a jiujitsu mat, and both gave me a competitive streak I still have. They also made me comfortable with losing, as long as I understood why.

Finance found me through school. At Coral Gables Senior High I competed in FBLA, which pulled me into accounting, and a teacher who took my questions seriously showed me that the numbers on a financial statement tell you how a business really works. From there I wanted to know which businesses were worth owning and why.

I spent my first semester of college in London, then came to Tallahassee and got involved with the Finance Society's Investment Banking and Alternatives group. This year I moved from learning about deals to working on them.

Where I've been working

May 2026 – Present

AMB Advisory Services

M&A Analyst · Tampa

This is where I worked my first live deal. We advised a buyer on a leveraged acquisition of a small business and its real estate, valued at up to $1.6M. I ran valuation comps on both the business and the property and worked through how rent increases and different term-loan structures changed the buyer's cash flow and equity return. Mar – May 2026

Sunshine Capital

Private Equity Intern · Tampa

Sunshine is a search fund acquiring small accounting firms, generally at 0.7x to 0.9x revenue. I sourced over 8,000 firms, reached out to owners, built comps, and pitched targets to the fund principal. Talking directly with owners who had spent decades building their practices showed me that selling a business is a personal decision as much as a financial one. It was my first real look at the buy side, and it taught me to judge a target by how well it fits the strategy, not just by its revenue.

What I've been learning

Things I keep thinking about

Small details move deal returns

On the AMB deal, I was surprised how much a rent step-up or a change in the loan term moved what the buyer walked away with. Working through those scenarios made leverage feel real to me, and it's a big reason I want to get better at LBO modeling.

What makes a target worth buying

Screening thousands of accounting firms taught me that the numbers are only the first cut. Owner count, headcount, location and niche services mattered just as much to whether a firm fit the fund's strategy.

Pitching tech stocks

As a Tech Group Analyst in FSU's Undergraduate Investment Fund, a 36-person team managing $13M, I'm learning to build a thesis from industry research, competitive analysis and regulation, then defend a valuation in front of the group.

What I'd bring to Recalc

I've seen small deals from the buy side at a search fund and from the advisory side at a brokerage. Both made me want to understand investing more deeply, and Recalc's training in accounting, business analysis and LBO modeling is exactly what I'm missing. FSU doesn't have a large alumni presence in finance, so a cohort and mentors who genuinely care would matter a lot to me.

Here is what I hope to bring back to the community:

  • A habit of catching up fast. I didn't start exploring a career in finance until late in my freshman year, and I was behind a lot of my peers. It wasn't the first time: I also started baseball later than most of my high school teammates and had to close that gap quickly. Both times, the extra work paid off.
  • What I've learned recruiting from a non-target. I'm still early in my career, but I've learned a lot about finding opportunities without a big alumni network, from cold outreach to leaning on mentors. I'd be glad to share what has worked for me and learn what has worked for others.
  • A commitment to giving back. I already mentor underclassmen at FSU, and I'd want to do the same for future Recalc cohorts.
